The Asked Method

How Every Question Earns Its Place

A brief is only as useful as the questions inside it. Asked builds each one from independent signals and labels every question with the evidence behind it. It also tells the writer what form each answer should take, so answer engines can lift and cite it. The full working logic of the tool is published below for anyone to inspect or build on.

The Method At A Glance

Signals from real search demand Predicted fan-out for B2B buyers Evidence labels on every question Predictions checked after publishing

AI search engines rarely answer a question by searching for it directly. Google describes how AI Mode breaks a question into subtopics and runs many searches at once on the user's behalf, then assembles one answer from the pages that address each part. Google's own engineers have explained that these sub-queries often show zero volume in traditional keyword tools. A keyword list built on search volume therefore misses most of what an answer engine looks for.

Google's explanation points to a second detail that shapes the method. Answer engines extract and cite individual chunks of a page, so a piece earns citations one section at a time and has to be planned one question at a time.

Search Demand

Asked collects the questions people search for in Google and Bing autocomplete around a keyword, expanded with question words and B2B modifiers such as pricing, integration and compliance. Autocomplete ranks phrases by popularity and gives no search volume, so it shows what buyers ask without claiming exact numbers.

Predicted Fan-Out

The built-in engine breaks the keyword down along the B2B evaluation path below, the way an answer engine would. With a free Gemini key, a language model does the same work with more nuance. Fan-out is generative, and the exact sub-queries change from run to run. Asked treats this signal as a prediction of the ground an answer engine is likely to cover, and never presents it as a record of an actual search.

Software buyers research in a fairly predictable order, and Asked follows it when predicting fan-out. Every keyword is tested against the facets below.

Fit And Function

What the solution is, and whether it suits a team of this size and type.

Cost

How vendors charge, by seat, usage or volume, and what that means for next year's bill.

Security And Compliance

The certifications and data residency terms a security review will ask to see, SOC 2 Type II among them.

Integration And Rollout

Whether it plugs into the current stack, and how many weeks implementation realistically takes.

Alternatives And Trade-Offs

Named competitors and head-to-head comparisons, along with the honest limitations of each option.

Proof And Outcomes

Benchmarks and case results, plus the return a buyer can take to budget sign-off.

A narrow technical keyword may open only a few facets, while a category keyword like data warehouse migration usually opens every one of them.

Every question in a brief carries a label, so the writer can see why it is there.

Confirmed - The question appears in real autocomplete and also falls inside a predicted fan-out facet. Confirmed questions form the backbone of the piece.

Search Demand - Buyers already search for the question, but it sits outside the predicted fan-out. It often deserves a short answer or an FAQ entry rather than a full section.

Predicted Fan-Out - The question follows the evaluation path, but nobody searches for it in those exact words yet. Competitors tend to miss these questions, so they carry the most upside and the most uncertainty.

Confirmed→ Search Demand→ Predicted Fan-Out

Questions are ordered first by label. Within each label, they follow the evaluation path from fit through to proof, so the ranked list doubles as a natural reading order for the finished piece. Near-duplicates are merged into one question, keeping the clearest phrasing.

Answer engines lift answers that match the shape of the question. Asked reads the wording of each question and assigns the format its answer should take.

Definition

What is and what does questions get a direct one or two sentence answer, placed first.

Process

How to and how do questions get numbered steps.

Comparison

Versus, alternative and best for questions get a side-by-side comparison on shared criteria.

Figure

How much, how long and how many questions get a specific number with its source.

Criteria

Which and what should questions get a short list of decision factors.

Conditional Answer

Can, does and is questions get a yes or no, followed by the condition that makes it true.

A method that cannot be tested is an opinion. After a piece goes live, users can compare its brief against free first-party data.

Bing Webmaster Tools - its AI Performance report shows citations across Copilot and partners, listed by the grounding queries that triggered them.

Google Search Console - its generative AI report shows how often pages appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode.

Asked matches those queries against the questions it predicted and shows which ones surfaced in real AI answers. Users who choose to share the result add it to an open dataset, and each label's track record is published as the data grows.

Being clear about limits is part of the method. Asked does not report search volume, because autocomplete only signals relative popularity. It cannot see the exact prompts people enter into AI assistants, and no public source offers them. Bing's citation report does not cover ChatGPT or other outside assistants, so validation covers only part of AI search. Fan-out is also generative, so a prediction describes likely ground and can never be a guaranteed list.
